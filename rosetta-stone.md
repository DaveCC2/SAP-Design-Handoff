## Company Overview

**Company Name:** Solving America's Problems (podcast)
**Industry:** Public Affairs / Civic Media
**Founded:** January 2025
**Headquarters:** Florida business entity (Miami roots); virtual operation. Jerremy based in Nevada.
**Leadership Team:** Dave Conley — Producer, Co-Host, Operations. Jerremy — Co-Host, on-mic lead, network.
**Core Focus:** A weekly podcast that turns real conversations about America's toughest issues into a movement — built by people who believe the best and brightest should serve their country, not just their careers.
**Tagline:** America's Opportunities: From Conversation to Candidacy

## 1. Vision, Mission, and Values

**Vision:** A springboard for freedom and change. The levers of power in America are siloed by special interest and money, and democracy is hindered by what people can buy and influence. We've lost the ability for meritocracy and goodness to seep through where everybody benefits. Civic duty and a lowercase nationalism have devolved. The tree of America needs tender care, fertilizer, and watering to continue for the next 250 years. Because money has such gravity around the ability to make change, the path is to build a big enough community and movement to enable Jerremy's vision of running for office — with a movement already started and excited around him, rather than the standard path of raising money, making promises, and selling your soul. High morals, high conviction, and drive can produce movements that create real change for a great country.

**Vision — both-and, not either/or:** Jerremy will definitely run, and the movement is key to that. It is not a choice between "he runs" and "the movement exists." Both are the point. The movement is what gets him there; running is the destination the movement is built to reach.

**Mission:** Today, it's about creating contacts for Jerremy, educating him beyond his finance world into politics, and raising his visibility in the broader conversation. The method: start with assumptions about a problem, let conversations reshape them, and end each series with a concrete pathway to solve a real, tangible issue in the United States.

**Core Values:**
- **Service** — the Kennedy ideal: the best and brightest belong in public life, not just finance or tech. "Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country." *Note on wording:* "not just finance or tech" is deliberate. We are not throwing shade on people with careers or ordinary jobs — plenty of people are happy with jobs and careers. The point is that public service is a *calling*, not that other paths are lesser. Do not sharpen this into an attack on working people.
- **Integrity** — truth to power, self-funded, unbought.
- **Authenticity** — real conversations, assumptions tested not defended.
- **Anti-tribalism** — no team jersey, no nose-holding, participation over apathy.

## 2. Identity and Positioning

**Elevator Pitch:** Solving America's Problems is the weekly podcast that turns real conversations about America's toughest issues into a movement — built by people who believe the best and brightest should serve their country, not just their careers.

**Brand Personality:** Professional yet approachable. Conversational, grounded, and real — long-form, plain-spoken, with intellectual depth underneath. We borrow the accessibility of the biggest podcasts but refuse the typecast: no team jersey, no pod-bro energy, no pigeonhole. If you can't quite put your finger on us, that's working. The goal isn't to sound like anyone — it's to create movement, and the end result is action. Jerremy brings the everyman warmth; Dave brings the intellectual rigor.

**Unique Selling Proposition:** We're the only podcast building a political movement on purpose — not exploring topics for entertainment, not politicians running a show, but turning real conversations into a pathway for someone to actually run for office, self-funded and unbought. We don't believe "politician" should be a job you write on your taxes. *Raw feeling behind it:* the fact that "politician" is a career you can put on a tax form is, to us, disgusting. Politics as a profession is the disease; the movement is the antidote. Capture the disgust, not just the clean line.

**Tagline:** America's Opportunities: From Conversation to Candidacy

**Tagline origin (do not revert):** "From Conversation to Candidacy" was chosen over "Where Service Beats Career" specifically because we do not want to throw shade on people with careers or jobs. The softened phrasing protects ordinary working people while still elevating service as a calling. Keep that intent intact.

**Guiding principle:** Talk is cheap — watch what people do, not what they say. The show is not just conversation; it is built to produce action. If a piece of content doesn't move someone toward participation, it isn't doing its job.

## 3. Offerings and Products

**Overview:** Weekly audio podcast (primary), weekly video spots at the intersection of current events, money, and politics, plus evergreen content. All free. No paid tier yet.

**Target Audiences:** Market band, not the avatar — men and women, roughly 35 and up, who feel disenfranchised by the two-party system and want real conversation over tribal noise. Secondary: people in finance and policy who already follow Jerremy. Write every piece to the one person in Section 4. The band is who else can overhear it.

**Pricing Philosophy:** Free access, self-funded by Jerremy, no special-interest money. Monetization only when it doesn't compromise independence. Future revenue likely sponsor-read ads done tactfully (Huberman model), possibly a paid tier once the movement is bigger.

## 4. Customer Avatar

**Jill.** One person. Not a range.

Early fifties, but looks and acts like she's in her forties. Runs a small business that's climbing toward medium-sized. No kids — didn't want them. Lives between the United States and internationally. Fit, fun, social; she thrives in rooms of other high-flyers like her. Smart, witty, politically minded — stays current on domestic and international affairs. Old-school conservative in the Pat Buchanan mold, but socially liberal, which puts her in the disenfranchised-Trump-supporter camp with aspirations rooted in her earliest political hope: Bill Clinton, back when she was first learning to vote. Her north star is Elizabeth McCord from *Madam Secretary* — a strong woman doing real good in politics with a real agenda. That character is who Jill aspires to be.

**Taste:** wide-ranging. Good dance music, 80s, cool Argentine guitar, upbeat pop with sophistication — a universal black dress of music, good for the day and the night. Loves good movies.

**Win-song** (when things are going well): the upbeat Argentine guitar, the dance floor, the universal black dress. She turns it up and celebrates.

**Loss-song** (when the country's going sideways): not sad or sappy — defiant. Something loud, driving, unapologetic. The anthem that says "I'm still here and I'm not done." She doesn't spiral; she turns the volume up.

**Listening rhythm:** The show publishes Monday through Thursday. Jill's real listening night is **Sunday night** — the quiet before the week starts, when the calendar is still hers and she can sit with a serious conversation without it competing with the workday. That's when the 3AM fear is closest to the surface and the 3PM logic is most open to being reached. Write the episode so it lands on a Sunday night: the conversation that makes her want to participate before Monday arrives.

**3PM logic** (what she will say out loud, and what copy may say back):
- Her voice does not matter in a system bought by money.
- She wants to engage but has no time for noise.
- She needs inspiration, not another lecture.
- She will give an hour to a serious conversation that does not demand a team jersey.

**3AM fear** (what wakes her; do not name this in public copy — talk into the 3PM so this is reached):
She is watching the country get purchased while she runs the company that was supposed to be her contribution. Civic duty feels like a costume other people wore. The hope she had as a young voter — Clinton, then the promise of something better — curdled into exactly the tribal noise she hates. If she keeps waiting for a clean opening in the calendar, she will look up and the window will be gone — and she will have been a competent spectator to a decline she still believes can be stopped. Her deepest fear is irrelevance: that the system gets bought while she was busy building something real.

**How to talk to her:** same facts, different words. Lead with the 3PM (time, noise, a conversation worth the hour). Let the 3AM sit underneath (irrelevance, a bought system, a country she still thinks is worth serving). Do not sell the fear. Sell the next honest conversation and the first concrete act of participation.

**Still missing (do not invent):** one favorite listener who already behaves like her. Lock that from a real person, not from the model.

**Anti-avatar:** the person who wants a team jersey, a lecture, or a pundit to think for them. Let them go.

## 7. Voice and Style Guide

**Tone:** Conversational, professional but not polished. Jerremy speaks from the heart — warm, inspirational, plain-spoken. Dave speaks from the head — systems-oriented, analytical, structured. Together they sound like two smart friends having a serious conversation that happens to matter.

Two distinct registers that collide on purpose. Jerremy runs hot — hype-man cadence, direct address to the audience, big declarative statements delivered with exclamation. Dave runs cold-analytical with a sarcastic undertow — he explains mechanisms, then editorializes with dry disbelief. Neither performs neutrality. The show works because Jerremy's optimism and Dave's skepticism are visibly in tension in real time, not smoothed into a shared "podcast voice."

**Jerremy's voice, in his own words:**
- Opens big and direct to camera: "Ladies and gentlemen, boys and girls, friends and family, team and fans from around the world" / "Hey, it's Monday... Do it. Do it. All right?"
- Runs sentences long, chaining clauses with "and," then breaks them with a short punch: "Opportunities never go away, ladies and gentlemen. They only switch places."
- Repeats a word three times for emphasis instead of intensifying it: "Beautiful, beautiful, beautiful." / "I love it. I love it."
- Treats numbers as texture, not just data — rattles off exact prices and dates mid-sentence like he's reading a ticker: "54,749.47" / "one seventy-five... one seventy-four... one sixty-four."
- Fillers: "you know," "I mean," "so," "ultimately," "kind of" — these smooth transitions rather than signal hesitation. He rarely restarts a sentence.
- Question style with guests: builds a runway before the ask, then lands a specific, sometimes blunt question — "What actually happens to your file when it lands on an underwriter's desk?" Closes interviews with a rapid-fire lightning round of short, punchy prompts ("Finish this sentence...", "True or false...").
- Emotional register: exclamatory, playful, uses nicknames for Dave ("my boy," "dude," "brother"), and drops in genuine enthusiasm markers — "Dope," "Nice," "Good grief," "Bananas" — that read as unscripted delight, not filler.

**Dave's voice, in his own words:**
- Physically stumbles into his sentences, and the stumble is part of the delivery, not noise to clean up: "I, I just, like why do we pay taxes?" / "I, I, I mean, I don't, I, I don't know economics, but I do know math, and that doesn't make any sense."
- Playful witty sarcasm as the default reaction to absurd numbers or official spin — deploys mock enthusiasm to puncture something: "That's ex- oh, I, I'm so excited. I got, I got a cake. I got party hats... but the entire cake was on fire."
- Narrates his own gestures because the audio can't carry them, which becomes a bit in itself: "[raises his hands going] 'Ugh.'" / "like the emoji that's like just, just, just the giant shrug."
- Ends explanatory runs with a tag question inviting agreement rather than a full stop: "...you know?" / "right?" — used constantly, almost a verbal comma.
- Question style: the "I don't wanna sound dumb, but" entry point into genuinely technical questions — "How do they make money? I, I don't wanna sound dumb on this, but is it, it is all advertising?" He asks how systems work, not just what happened.
- Turns personal on request, briefly and without dwelling — the divorced/widower home-buying detail surfaces once, plainly, then moves on. No performance of vulnerability, just a fact dropped into the analysis.
- Capable of real, unhedged anger when a policy detail lands on ordinary people — the paid-sick-leave riff builds from stat to stat until it lands on a gut-punch line: "Think about that, expectant mothers. Think about seven days of sick leave to have your child."

**Writing Tips:** Long-form, real, no pod-bro energy, no tribal framing. Questions should test assumptions, not perform certainty. Disagreement is welcome; typecasting is not.

**Writing Tips (for scripts, show notes, social copy):**
- Don't flatten the two voices into one house style. If a line is written "for Jerremy," it should be able to run long and land on a short punch. If a line is written "for Dave," it should sound like someone thinking out loud and arriving somewhere sharper than the setup suggested.
- Real numbers over rounded ones. Both hosts cite exact figures ($54,749.47, not "almost 55K"; 45% more expensive, not "way more") — precision reads as credibility, not pedantry.
- Let a joke sit rather than explaining it. The $40T/birthday-cake bit works because neither host clarifies the joke — Jerremy escalates it, Dave answers straight.
- Tag questions ("right?" "you know?") are fine in transcript-adjacent copy (show notes, pull quotes) — they're part of the vocal signature, not a flaw to edit out.
- Playful witty sarcasm is allowed and expected from Dave's side, especially aimed at institutional non-answers (a CFO's "psychological impact of gas prices" line got a direct, unhedged reaction). Keep it aimed at power, not at guests or listeners.

## 8. Company Culture

**Leadership Style:** Dave runs operations and sets the agenda; Jerremy leads on-mic and brings his network. The gap is collaboration — Jerremy is responsive when pulled in, but the default has drifted toward Dave carrying everything. Fix: a weekly 15–20 minute check-in aimed at "what you actually want this to become," not task updates. Quarterly in-person resets (2–3 days) keep the relationship grounded.

**Internal Interactions:** Dave preps, produces, and publishes; Jerremy shows up ready to talk. The check-in exists to keep Jerremy's vision in the room, not just his voice on the mic.

**Core Values in Action:** Service means the best conversations, not the easiest ones.

## 9. AI Philosophy

**AI Role:** Probabilistic by nature — excellent at finding holes, surfacing opportunities, and broadening thinking where human expertise is thin. Terrible at binary calls, so those stay human. Used everywhere as a co-producer, with explicit guardrails so it proposes and you decide. Fills gaps where you have no expertise (e.g., social media) and frees your time to focus on judgment and direction.

**Ethical Stance:** Self-funded, unbought, AI serves the mission. Guardrails are non-negotiable — AI widens the map, humans choose the road.

**Vision for the Future:** Bots running more of the back end autonomously, system getting less fragile, but the judgment layer never automated.

## 10. Long-Term Goals and Strategy

**One Year (Process):** Infrastructure — website, mailing list, automated back end, social media dialed in. The show stops depending on you steering every piece.

**Two Years (Traction):** The community is active, Jerremy's name is known beyond finance, and the first real test of whether people will show up for something.

**Three Years (Result):** Jerremy's running, self-funded, with a base that already believes in him.

**Success Metrics:** Listeners and community members, not just downloads. Contacts converted into movement participants. Jerremy's visibility outside the money world. And eventually, a campaign that doesn't need to sell its soul.
