**Keep**

**Question / move**
- Tagline is too long for a first-time visitor. Do not lead with the full tagline. Need a 1–3 second hook that makes someone scroll.
- Guest-range line (one CEO, one everyday person, one expert) — keep the idea, but not this prominent right up front. It belongs lower or quieter.
- Role labels: do not say "heart" / "systems" in the corners. Use the real roles: Dave = co-host and producer; Jerremy = co-host and candidate. Candidate ties to the community-to-candidacy arc — keep that as Jerremy's role.
- Faces must not be blocked or cropped away. Real headshots, visible.

**Kill**
- The long tagline as the lead element. No hook in the first seconds.

**Verdict (Dave, one sentence):** What landed was the subscribe button as a keep in concept; the question mark is on the placement of the guest range; the kill is the really long thing — we need a hook that catches somebody in one to three seconds, and I have not seen that on this page.

**For Grok Build round two:** Preserve the lamp feeling, the single-column quiet, the gold subscribe button, the top wordmark treatment, and the warm/cool host-photo pairing with honest role labels. Shorten the lead to a hook. Demote the guest-range reassurance. Do not reintroduce the dead phrase. Never cover faces.

# Round-two brief for Grok Build

**Goal:** Fifteen new visual treatments that move us closer to the live site. Not a remix of Sunday Night. Same locked sentence, same single "Start listening" button, same stone v1.5. Only the room changes.

**The one question every treatment must answer:** What stops the scroll in one to three seconds?

**Must include:**
- A real hook in the first screen — above the sentence, before the button. The hook is the unsolved problem; solve it.
- The locked H1 verbatim: "The weekly podcast that turns real conversations about America's toughest issues into a movement — built by people who believe the best and brightest should serve their country, not just their careers."
- Button copy locked: **Start listening**. One button above the fold. No email, no icon row.
- Real headshots of both hosts, fully visible, never blocked or cropped. Faces are the mechanism — people respond to us.
- Honest role labels: Dave = co-host and producer; Jerremy = co-host and candidate. No personality-corner labels ("heart," "cold analytical," "systems").
- Warm Jerremy / cool Dave pairing as a deliberate design choice — generous, never a jab.
- Visual-first hero. No walls of text. Grab the eye, then earn the words.
- The funnel: subscribe is the only ask above the fold. "When you're ready" block below — newsletter, social, resources. Quiet, secondary.
- "This week" as latest episode. "Before you ask" (not FAQ) anchored to the community-to-candidacy arc.
- Mobile-first. Phone layout must work, not just desktop.
- Bold, rich colors are on the table — tuned to Jill, not to my taste. Design figures out what she likes.

**Must not include:**
- Dead phrases: "Service over career," "Where Service Beats Career," "beat your career." No shade on people with jobs.
- Guests or proof above the sentence.
- One-host giant images. Both-and: two people, not one.
- Desktop-only layouts.
- Corporate, dated (2011-era), phallic, childlike, or masculine treatments that miss Jill.
- The full tagline as the lead.
- Auto-advancing carousels (accessibility).

**Reference treatments to study (not copy):**
- Grok 01 Sunday Night — the baseline. Quiet, adult, funnel-correct, verbatim sentence.
- Grok 02 Shop Sign — the carousel concept for the guest/proof strip.
- Grok 07 — eye-drawing to the single act; big-words hook experiment.
- Grok 11 Hot and Cold — split-tone metaphor, done clean.
- Grok 03 Two Chairs — faces work; empty chairs are decoration.
- Claude 1A / 1C — wordmark treatment, warm/cool pairing (Claude found it first).

**Output per treatment (same as round one):**
- Feeling
- Layout logic
- Key visual choice
- Palette
- Type
- Hard-fail check
- Phone + desktop still
- One-line note: what is the hook, and does it stop the scroll?

**Dependency before round two ships:** Transparent-border logo via Grok Imagine (from Claude 1B note).
